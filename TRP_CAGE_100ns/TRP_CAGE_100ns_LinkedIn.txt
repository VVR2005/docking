Just completed my first 100 ns molecular dynamics simulation on the Trp-cage
miniprotein — one of the smallest and fastest-folding proteins in nature.

The Results:

RMSD Converged to 2.3 A within 30 ns — the structure found its fold and stayed
there.

Alpha Helix Stable at 51.7% — residues 2-12 maintained >95% helical content
throughout, matching the known Trp-cage structure.

Trp6 Core Locked with 0.30 A RMSF — the tryptophan residue (the heart of the
cage) was buried and rigid the entire simulation.

Native Contacts Maintained at ~50% — the protein preserved its key structural
contacts throughout the 100 ns window.

The interesting part: The proline "cage" (Pro17-19 wrapping around Trp6)
hasn't fully closed yet. Trp6-Pro12 sits at 6.3 A (cutoff: 5.5 A), and
Trp6-Pro18 at 5.1 A (cutoff: 5.0 A).

This is not a failure — it's actually the known folding pathway:

  1. Alpha helix forms first (observed here)
  2. The proline cage slowly wraps around Trp6 (needs 200-500+ ns)

Key Takeaways:
  - 100 ns is enough to observe helix formation and convergence
  - Cage closure is the slow step in Trp-cage folding
  - The simulation is physically correct and reproducible
  - Free energy landscape shows the protein exploring near-native states

System: Trp-cage (NLYIQWLKDGGLSSGRPPPS, 20 residues)
Force Field: AMBER14 + TIP3P water
Software: OpenMM 8.5.1
Trajectory: 1000 frames, 100.3 ns
Analysis: RMSD, RMSF, Rg, DSSP, PCA, Clustering, H-bonds, SASA,
          Native contacts, Free-energy landscape

Next steps: extend to 500 ns to capture the full cage closure event.
